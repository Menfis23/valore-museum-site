Myanmar Gallery.

Bagan's golden temples, thanaka, and the many peoples — Bamar, Karen, Chin, Rohingya — who call it home.

A Land of Golden Temples and Many Peoples.

Myanmar's Buddhist civilization reached extraordinary heights at Bagan, where kings commissioned more than 4,000 temples and stupas between the 11th and 13th centuries — some 2,000 still stand along the Ayeyarwady River today, one of the greatest concentrations of religious architecture on Earth. But "Myanmar," or "Burma," has never meant one people: the Bamar majority shares the country with the Karen, Shan, Chin, Kachin, Mon, Rakhine, Rohingya, and dozens of other groups, together speaking well over 100 languages — a diversity the military-ruled state has, at different points, tried to erase rather than celebrate.

Everyday Life & Art.

Thanaka, a pale paste ground from tree bark, is worn on the face by women and children as both sunscreen and beauty tradition, often applied in leaf or circle patterns. Men and women alike wear the longyi, a wrapped ankle-length garment suited to the tropical heat, and puppetry (yoke thé) has narrated Buddhist and royal court stories for centuries through marionettes carved and strung by hand. Karen communities, many Christian since 19th-century missionary contact, carry their own textile traditions — handwoven garments in deep reds and blacks — alongside harvest festivals and a distinct written script older than colonial rule.

Beyond the Headlines.

Since the late 1980s, ethnic minorities including the Karen, Chin, and Rohingya have faced decades of armed conflict and persecution by Myanmar's military; hundreds of thousands of Karen refugees spent years, sometimes decades, in camps along the Thai border, while the Rohingya — denied citizenship altogether — have faced what the UN has called a campaign of genocide since 2017. Myanmar's Burmese-origin community is now one of the largest refugee-origin populations in Allegheny County, and its members carry Bagan's golden spires, thanaka's quiet ritual, and a resilience shaped by generations of displacement into their new home.
