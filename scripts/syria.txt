Some of the oldest cities on Earth, and centuries of craft and coexistence.

Cradle of Cities.

Damascus and Aleppo are among the oldest continuously inhabited cities on Earth, sitting at the crossroads of the ancient Silk Road where caravans carried silk, spices, and ideas between Asia, Africa, and Europe for thousands of years. The Old City of Damascus, the Roman ruins of Palmyra, and the Crusader-era fortress of Krak des Chevaliers stand as layers of that history — Roman, Byzantine, Umayyad, Ottoman — built one on top of the other.

Everyday Life & Art.

Damascene artisans have inlaid wood with mother-of-pearl and brass for centuries, a craft called khatam that still furnishes homes today. Aleppo soap, made from olive and laurel oil through a process passed down for generations, is still cured for months before use. Syrian tables are built around dishes like kibbeh and muhammara, and generosity with guests is treated as a point of honor — food is never served in small portions.

Beyond the Headlines.

Under the Umayyad Caliphate, Damascus was the capital of an empire stretching from Spain to Central Asia, and later became a center of the Islamic Golden Age's advances in mathematics, astronomy, and medicine. For centuries, Christian, Muslim, Druze, Kurdish, and Armenian communities have lived alongside one another in Syria's cities — a diversity that Syrian diaspora communities abroad continue to carry forward through preserved crafts, cuisine, and music.
