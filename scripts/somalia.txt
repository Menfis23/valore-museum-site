Somalia Gallery.

A nation of poets whose oral epics carried law and history for centuries, and ancient Indian Ocean trading ports.

A Nation of Poets.

Long before Somali became a written language in 1972, poetry carried the nation's history, law, and politics — elders composed and memorized epic gabay verses that could take hours to recite, used to settle disputes, rally clans, or mourn the dead, earning Somalia its reputation as a "nation of poets." For over a thousand years before that, Somali coastal city-states like Zeila and Mogadishu were major Indian Ocean trading ports, linking the Horn of Africa to Arabia, Persia, and India in networks of cloth, ivory, and incense — a cosmopolitan history that predates the 1991 collapse of the Somali state by many centuries.

Everyday Life & Art.

Camel herding still shapes much of Somali life and language — Somali has dozens of words describing a camel's age, color, and temperament — and nomadic families build the aqal, a dome-shaped portable house, so it can be dismantled, loaded onto camels, and rebuilt at the next grazing ground. Henna painting marks weddings and Eid celebrations, and hospitality is built around cups of shaah (spiced tea) shared with guests as a matter of course. Somali Bantu communities — descendants of people brought from southeastern Africa generations ago, historically marginalized within Somali clan society — carry their own distinct agricultural traditions and languages, including Kizigua, alongside broader Somali culture.

Beyond the Headlines.

Since the central government collapsed in 1991, decades of civil war, famine, and more recently drought intensified by climate change have displaced millions of Somalis, hundreds of thousands of whom passed through Kenya's Dadaab refugee camp — one of the largest and longest-running refugee camps in the world. Somali Bantu refugees, formally recognized by the U.S. as a persecuted population in 1999, were among the first large groups resettled, and Somali-origin families are now well established in Pittsburgh and cities across the country, carrying forward a poetic tradition, a trading history, and a resilience far older than the war that displaced them.
